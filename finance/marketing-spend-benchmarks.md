# Marketing Spend Benchmarks: E-commerce and Health Supplements

*Compiled 2026-10-21 for Circady Health planning. Percentages are the same in any currency. Dollar figures are USD as reported by each source.*

## TL;DR

| Benchmark | Marketing as % of revenue | Source quality |
|---|---|---|
| Public DTC/CPG median (12 companies, 10-K data) | **~13.3%** (range 2–31%) | Public filings, compiled by an agency |
| DTC brands with under $1M revenue (ad spend only) | **25–35%+** | Agency benchmark |
| DTC brands at $1–5M | **20–30%** | Agency benchmark |
| DTC brands at $5–10M | **15–25%** | Agency benchmark |
| Supplement DTC brands (average) | **~32%** | Agency claim from one underlying source |
| Beauty and supplements at $5–25M | **22–28%** | Agency benchmark |
| "Top-decile" supplement brand model | **20%** (65% GM, then 35% contribution, then 27% EBITDA) | Operator newsletter |
| Hims & Hers (telehealth and wellness DTC), FY2025 | **~39%** (FY2024: ~46%) | 10-K; % is my calculation |
| Thorne (premium supplements), FY2021 | **~13.6%** (FY2020: ~8.1%) | 10-K; % is my calculation |
| Grove Collaborative (DTC consumables), FY2025 | **~5.6%** (advertising only) | 10-K figure; % calculated by a third party |

**What this means for an early-stage supplement brand:** expect marketing to take **25–40% of revenue** in the first years. It should fall toward **15–20%** as repeat and subscription revenue builds. Brands with heavy paid acquisition, like Hims, stay near 40%. Brands driven by practitioners and wholesale, like Thorne, run 8–15%.

## Tenengroup

Tenengroup is a private Israeli e-commerce company based in Tel Aviv/Ramat Gan and founded in 2007. It describes itself as a "data driven, end-to-end ecommerce house" with 30+ DTC brands, mostly personalized jewelry. It runs its own platform and an in-house performance marketing team, and buys ads across Google, Meta and Amazon.

- **It does not publish financials.** Revenue estimates from data aggregators disagree, from about $58M to $250–500M, so none of them are reliable.
- **No marketing-% figure exists publicly.** As a proxy, use the ad-spend-by-stage table above. A performance-marketing portfolio like Tenengroup's typically sits at the high end of each band, around 25–35%. It buys revenue through paid social and search and has little repeat or subscription revenue. Real figures would require PitchBook or D&B access, or asking the company directly.

## Supplement-specific context

- **Gross margin:** public supplement pure-plays run about 71–80% gross margin but only about 5% operating margin. Acquisition and overhead consume most of the gap.
- **Contribution margin:** a healthy brand clears 55–65% contribution margin on a first order before marketing, and contribution after marketing must stay positive.
- **CAC:** blended CAC is about $80–130 (median about $89). The best brands run $40–60 through creative, affiliate and referral flywheels.
- **LTV:CAC:** at least 3:1, and 4:1 at maturity. Payback takes 3–6 months, or 1–3 months on subscription cohorts.
- **Repeat rate:** a 90-day repeat rate of 20–30%+ is described as healthy.
- **MER (revenue ÷ total marketing):** at 75%+ gross margin, the minimum healthy blended MER is 2.0–2.5x. That works out to marketing at 40–50% of revenue as a ceiling, not a target.

## Applying it to Circady (pre-revenue)

At zero revenue, a % of revenue does not apply yet. Budget marketing from the cash raised and judge it against **CAC and repeat rate**, because those are what the benchmarks above depend on.

- **Months 0–6:** treat spend as tests. Track CAC against the $89 median, first-order contribution margin, and the 60–90-day repeat rate.
- **Once revenue starts:** aim to land in the 25–35% band within the first year. Move toward 20% as subscriptions and repeat orders build.
- **Kill or continue rule** (the "April if no revenue" checkpoint): define it in advance as specific CAC and repeat-rate thresholds, not only "any revenue".

## Caveats

- Most supplement-specific percentages come from agencies and consultancies, mainly Eightx and MHI Media, not audited data. The ~32% supplement figure traces back to one source.
- Hims & Hers and Thorne percentages are my own calculations from 10-K line items and aggregator revenue figures. Check them against the filings before citing them externally.
- "Marketing" means different things across sources: paid ads only, total marketing, or including staff. Compare like with like.
